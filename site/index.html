    Common UPI scams

    Most UPI fraud tricks you into approving a payment yourself. These are the patterns to know.

    “Receive money” requests

    You are told to enter your PIN or approve a request to receive money. A PIN is only ever needed to send money.

    Fake KYC or account blocked

    A message says your account will be closed unless you click a link and verify now.

    Prize, cashback and refund offers

    You are promised a reward if you scan a QR code or share a code first.

    Remote-access apps

    A caller asks you to install a screen-sharing app so they can “help”. They then see your OTPs and banking apps.

    Look-alike links

    Web addresses with extra words, odd spellings or numbers imitate real banks and wallets.

    Fake customer care numbers

    Numbers found through search results or social media are often run by scammers. Use the number printed on your card or in the official app.

    If you already paid a scammer

    1. Call 1930, India’s national cyber fraud helpline, as soon as you can. Quick reports give the best chance of stopping the money.

    2. Report online at cybercrime.gov.in.

    3. Call your bank using the number on your card or official app, and ask them to block your cards and UPI.

    4. Keep proof. Save screenshots, transaction IDs and phone numbers.

    Questions

    Does a low score mean it is safe?

    No. A low score only means we found no common warning signs. Always verify who is asking for money through a channel you trust.

    Do you store my messages?

    This website does not save anything. Your text or image is sent to the checking service only when you press Check now.

    Is this an AI model?

    The checker uses a set of rules for known scam patterns. It does not replace your own judgement or your bank’s advice.

    Will anyone ever ask for my UPI PIN or OTP?

    No. Your bank, UPI apps and genuine companies never ask for your PIN or OTP.
